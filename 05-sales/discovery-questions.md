# Sales Discovery Questions

**Status:** Draft v1  
**Owner:** Ameen Sales / Product  
**Audience:** Founder, sales lead, consultants, demo team

---

## Purpose

This document gives Ameen a structured discovery process for customer conversations.

The goal is not to sell immediately. The goal is to understand:

- The customer's compliance pressure.
- The frameworks they care about.
- The current process.
- The pain level.
- The data sensitivity constraints.
- The buying process.
- Whether Ameen should offer a pilot.

---

## Discovery Rule

Do not start with a product demo.

Start by understanding the customer's current compliance reality.

Ameen should sound like a trusted advisor, not a software salesperson.

---

## Conversation Opening

Suggested opening:

> Before showing the platform, I would like to understand how your team currently prepares for compliance reviews, where the pain is, and what would make a pilot successful for you.

---

## Section 1: Organization Context

1. What type of organization are you?
2. How large is the technology or cybersecurity environment?
3. Which teams are involved in compliance preparation?
4. Who owns cybersecurity compliance today?
5. Who owns evidence collection?
6. Who approves final compliance status?
7. Do you have a dedicated GRC function?
8. Do you rely mostly on internal teams, consultants, or both?

---

## Section 2: Framework Pressure

1. Which Saudi frameworks are currently most important for you?
2. Are you preparing for ECC, CCC, CSCC, TCC, OSMACC, DCC, DGA requirements, PDPL, SAMA requirements, or another framework?
3. Is this preparation connected to an internal review, external audit, regulatory request, board request, or consultant engagement?
4. What is the deadline?
5. What happens if the organization is not ready by that deadline?
6. Which framework causes the most work today?
7. Are you assessing one entity or multiple entities?
8. Do you need Arabic, English, or both?

---

## Section 3: Current Process

1. How do you currently collect policies and evidence?
2. Are you using spreadsheets, shared folders, GRC tools, ticketing systems, or consultant templates?
3. How do you map documents to controls today?
4. Who verifies whether a document is sufficient?
5. How do you track gaps?
6. How do you assign gap owners?
7. How do you report readiness to leadership?
8. How often is the readiness status updated?
9. What part of the current process is most painful?

---

## Section 4: Documents and Evidence

1. How many policies and procedures do you currently maintain?
2. Where are they stored?
3. Are documents mostly Arabic, English, or mixed?
4. Are documents standardized or inconsistent across departments?
5. Do you have evidence already collected for controls?
6. Is evidence stored centrally or scattered across teams?
7. Which evidence is hardest to collect?
8. Do you have sensitive documents that cannot leave your environment?

---

## Section 5: AI Readiness and Data Sensitivity

1. Are you allowed to use external AI services with internal documents?
2. Are there categories of documents that must stay inside your environment?
3. Would a local analyzer/private deployment be required for a pilot?
4. Who must approve AI usage?
5. Do you have internal AI governance requirements?
6. Would the organization accept AI suggestions if humans approve final results?
7. What level of explanation is required for AI-generated mappings?

---

## Section 6: Decision Process

1. Who is the business owner for this initiative?
2. Who is the technical approver?
3. Who is the compliance or audit approver?
4. Who signs the purchase order?
5. Is procurement involved early?
6. Is a security review required before pilot?
7. Is legal review required before pilot?
8. What budget category would this fall under: software, cybersecurity, compliance, consulting, or managed services?

---

## Section 7: Pilot Qualification

1. Would you prefer a limited pilot before a full rollout?
2. What framework should the pilot focus on?
3. How many documents should be included?
4. Which department or entity should be used as the pilot scope?
5. Who will review AI suggestions during the pilot?
6. What would make the pilot successful?
7. What output would leadership need to see?
8. What concerns must be resolved before moving forward?

---

## Section 8: Success Criteria

Ask the customer:

> At the end of a 60 to 90 day pilot, what would convince you that Ameen is worth adopting?

Possible success criteria:

- Faster document review
- Better visibility of gaps
- Clearer evidence requirements
- Easier executive reporting
- Reduced dependency on spreadsheets
- Stronger Arabic document handling
- Better audit preparation
- Better consultant productivity

---

## Red Flags

Be careful if the customer says:

- They expect Ameen to guarantee compliance.
- They expect regulator approval.
- They want to upload highly sensitive production data without security review.
- They have no owner for the pilot.
- They have no framework priority.
- They want broad enterprise production immediately before a controlled pilot.
- They will not allocate reviewers to validate AI results.

These customers may need education before commercial engagement.

---

## Strong Fit Signals

Ameen is a strong fit when:

- The customer has a clear compliance deadline.
- Evidence is scattered.
- Current assessment is spreadsheet-heavy.
- Arabic documents are important.
- Leadership wants visibility.
- Consultants are expensive or overloaded.
- Customer accepts human-reviewed AI assistance.
- Customer is open to controlled pilot.

---

## Closing Question

End discovery with:

> Based on what you shared, I recommend a controlled pilot focused on one framework and a limited document set. The goal would be to prove whether Ameen can reduce manual assessment effort and improve readiness visibility without creating risk. Would that be a reasonable next step?
