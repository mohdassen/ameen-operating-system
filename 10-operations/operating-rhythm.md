# Ameen Operating Rhythm

## Document Status

Owner: CEO / Product / Operations  
Applies to: Internal operating model  
Version: 1.0  
Status: Draft operating standard

---

## Purpose

This document defines the weekly and monthly rhythm for running Ameen as a serious product company.

Ameen must not operate as random conversations, scattered decisions, and disconnected engineering tasks. Ameen must operate through a clear rhythm that connects product, engineering, sales, customer feedback, security, and commercial readiness.

---

## Operating Principle

Ameen must be run as a trust-first enterprise software company.

The operating rhythm must answer six questions continuously:

1. Are we building the right product?
2. Is the product trustworthy enough to sell?
3. Are customers understanding the value?
4. Are pilots moving forward?
5. Are legal/security/commercial risks controlled?
6. What is the next highest-value action?

## Weekly Operating Rhythm

### 1. Product and Engineering Review

Frequency: Weekly  
Duration: 60 minutes

Agenda:

1. Product progress.
2. Engineering progress.
3. QA status.
4. Security risks.
5. Arabic AI quality status.
6. Framework content status.
7. Customer journey issues.
8. Next engineering priority.

Required output:

- Updated engineering priority.
- Product decisions logged.
- Risks updated.

---

### 2. Commercial Readiness Review

Frequency: Weekly  
Duration: 45 minutes

Agenda:

1. Website status.
2. Sales deck status.
3. Pilot pipeline.
4. Pricing assumptions.
5. Customer objections.
6. Legal/commercial blockers.
7. Next customer-facing asset.

Required output:

- Updated GTM checklist.
- Customer-facing gaps identified.
- Next sales asset assigned.

---

### 3. Pilot Health Review

Frequency: Weekly during active pilots  
Duration: 45 minutes

Agenda:

1. Customer status.
2. Documents uploaded.
3. Mapping quality.
4. Evidence progress.
5. Support issues.
6. Customer feedback.
7. Expansion potential.
8. Risks to renewal or conversion.

Required output:

- Pilot health status: Green, Amber, Red.
- Next actions.
- Customer decisions required.

---

### 4. Decision Log Review

Frequency: Weekly  
Duration: 15 minutes

Agenda:

1. New decisions made this week.
2. Decisions needing documentation.
3. Decisions needing reversal or clarification.
4. Decisions that affect product, sales, legal, or engineering.

Required output:

- Decision log updated.

---

## Monthly Operating Rhythm

### 1. Strategy Review

Frequency: Monthly  
Duration: 90 minutes

Agenda:

1. Are we still focused on the right market?
2. Are Saudi-first and trust-first still guiding decisions?
3. Which customer segment is showing strongest pull?
4. Which competitor risk is increasing?
5. Which feature is becoming commercially necessary?
6. Which planned feature should be delayed?

Required output:

- Roadmap adjustment.
- Commercial positioning update.
- Top risks updated.

---

### 2. Product Quality Review

Frequency: Monthly  
Duration: 90 minutes

Agenda:

1. QA pass rate.
2. Security posture.
3. Pen test status.
4. Arabic AI parity.
5. Framework content quality.
6. Customer-reported defects.
7. Readiness output reliability.

Required output:

- Quality scorecard.
- Risk acceptance decisions.
- Engineering hardening priorities.

---

### 3. Revenue and Pipeline Review

Frequency: Monthly  
Duration: 60 minutes

Agenda:

1. Active prospects.
2. Pilot proposals.
3. Closed pilots.
4. Pilot-to-paid conversion.
5. Pricing feedback.
6. Partner opportunities.
7. Revenue forecast.

Required output:

- Pipeline update.
- Pricing lessons.
- Sales next actions.

## Daily Execution Standard

Ameen does not need a heavy daily meeting in the early stage.

Instead, each workday should answer:

- What was completed?
- What is blocked?
- What risk changed?
- What is the next highest-value task?

This can be maintained in a short project log.

## Go/No-Go Discipline

Ameen must not move to a stronger commercial stage unless the evidence supports it.

### Pilot Ready

Allowed when:

- Customer journey works.
- Scope is controlled.
- Data handling is clear.
- AI limitations are disclosed.
- Pilot agreement exists.

### Enterprise Production Ready

Allowed only when:

- Encryption and access controls are adequate.
- Security review is complete.
- Legal review is complete.
- Framework content is reviewed.
- Support model is operational.
- Customer data model is safe.

---

## Final Standard

Ameen's operating rhythm must prevent chaos.

The company should move fast, but only in a controlled way that protects trust.
