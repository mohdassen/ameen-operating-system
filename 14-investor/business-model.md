# Ameen Business Model

## Purpose

This document defines the working business model for Ameen.

It is a planning document, not a financial promise. All pricing, margins, customer acquisition assumptions, and cost assumptions must be validated through pilots and customer conversations.

## Business Model Summary

Ameen should use a hybrid enterprise SaaS and professional-services model during the early stage.

The product should be sold as a platform, but early customers will still need onboarding, framework configuration, evidence guidance, and executive reporting support.

The goal is to use services to accelerate trust and adoption, not to become a pure consulting company.

## Revenue Streams

### 1. Paid Pilot Program

Paid pilots are the first commercial step.

Purpose:

- Validate product value.
- Produce case studies.
- Learn real customer workflows.
- Build trust before enterprise rollout.
- Convert pilots into annual contracts.

Pilot pricing should depend on scope, sensitivity, deployment model, number of entities, and support effort.

### 2. Annual Platform Subscription

After pilot validation, customers should convert to annual contracts.

Possible subscription dimensions:

- Number of legal entities or organizations.
- Number of frameworks.
- Number of documents or evidence records.
- Deployment model.
- Support level.
- Required integrations.

Avoid user-seat pricing as the primary model because compliance value is organizational, not only user-based.

### 3. Dedicated Deployment

Some customers, especially regulated or sensitive organizations, may require dedicated deployment.

Dedicated deployment can command higher pricing because it provides stronger isolation, better data control, and clearer AI/data-handling boundaries.

Possible models:

- Customer-controlled infrastructure.
- Private cloud deployment.
- Single-tenant hosted deployment.
- On-premise or restricted network deployment where technically feasible.

### 4. Professional Services

Professional services should support the platform and include:

- Onboarding.
- Initial readiness setup.
- Document inventory support.
- Evidence mapping workshops.
- Executive readiness reporting.
- Training.
- Framework configuration.

Professional services should not become the core product identity.

### 5. Consultant and Partner Model

Ameen can sell through consultants and managed service providers.

Partner revenue options:

- Partner licenses.
- Revenue share.
- Certified implementation partners.
- Consultant workspaces.
- Co-branded readiness reports.

Consultants should see Ameen as a force multiplier, not as a threat.

## Packaging Strategy

### Pilot

For early design partners.

Includes:

- Limited-scope deployment.
- Defined number of documents.
- Defined assessment scope.
- Local analyzer by default where sensitivity requires it.
- Executive readiness output.
- Feedback sessions.

### Professional

For medium organizations that need structured readiness without complex deployment.

Includes:

- Core document analysis.
- Core readiness dashboard.
- Limited frameworks.
- Standard support.

### Enterprise

For large organizations.

Includes:

- Multiple organizations or business units.
- More frameworks.
- Advanced evidence management.
- Role-based access.
- Extended audit trail.
- Higher support level.

### Government / Regulated

For sensitive customers.

Includes:

- Dedicated deployment options.
- Stronger data-handling controls.
- Local analyzer first.
- Formal governance reviews.
- Custom support and onboarding.

### Partner

For consulting firms and managed service providers.

Includes:

- Multi-client workspace.
- Reusable templates.
- Proposal and report support.
- Partner enablement.

## Cost Drivers

Ameen must track the following cost drivers:

- AI provider usage when external models are enabled.
- Hosting and storage.
- Customer support time.
- Professional services delivery time.
- Security reviews.
- Legal review.
- Framework expert review.
- Sales cycle effort.

External AI usage must not be uncapped in shared deployments.

## Early-Stage Commercial Rule

Until cost and risk are validated, Ameen should sell controlled pilots and dedicated deployments rather than open self-service usage.

## Gross Margin Objective

Long term, the platform should behave like SaaS with strong gross margins.

Early stage will have lower margins because onboarding, trust-building, expert review, and customer support will be heavier.

The target should be to productize repeated services over time.

## Land and Expand Model

Ameen should land with one urgent readiness use case, then expand.

Land:

- One entity.
- One or two frameworks.
- Limited document set.
- Executive readiness report.

Expand:

- More frameworks.
- More departments.
- More evidence owners.
- More entities.
- Partner/auditor collaboration.
- Continuous readiness monitoring.

## Pricing Principles

Pricing should follow these principles:

- Price based on organizational value, not only technical usage.
- Keep pilot pricing simple.
- Do not publish complex pricing too early.
- Use annual contracts for enterprise customers.
- Charge more for dedicated deployment and high-touch support.
- Protect margins when external AI is used.
- Avoid unlimited promises before usage data exists.

## What Ameen Should Not Do Early

Ameen should not:

- Offer free unlimited production use.
- Promise regulator certification.
- Sell to highly sensitive regulated production environments before security readiness.
- Build custom consulting projects that do not improve the product.
- Expand to every framework before ECC/pilot trust is proven.

## Business Model Thesis

> Ameen earns trust through controlled readiness pilots, converts customers to annual platform contracts, and expands into a Saudi compliance intelligence platform supported by partners and dedicated deployment options.
