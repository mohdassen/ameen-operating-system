# Ameen Channel Strategy

**Document owner:** Business / GTM  
**Status:** Draft v1  
**Purpose:** Define how Ameen can reach the Saudi market through direct sales, consulting partners, managed service providers, and technology alliances.

---

## 1. Strategic Position

Ameen should not depend only on direct sales.

Saudi compliance buying is trust-driven. Many customers already rely on consultants, system integrators, managed service providers, auditors, and technology vendors. Ameen should use these trusted relationships without losing product control.

The channel strategy is:

> Sell directly where trust is needed. Partner where access is faster.

---

## 2. Channel Priorities

### Priority 1: Direct Founder-Led Sales

Best for the first 5-10 pilot customers.

Purpose:
- Validate product value.
- Learn real buyer objections.
- Control messaging.
- Build early references.
- Avoid partner distortion before the story is mature.

Target buyers:
- Government entities.
- Universities.
- Healthcare organizations.
- Medium and large Saudi enterprises.
- Consulting firms that need acceleration tools.

### Priority 2: Consulting Partners

Consulting partners can use Ameen to deliver faster readiness assessments.

They are not just resellers. They become Ameen-powered advisors.

Best partner types:
- Cybersecurity consulting firms.
- GRC consulting firms.
- NCA readiness consultants.
- Digital transformation advisory firms.
- Audit support firms.

### Priority 3: Managed Service Providers

MSPs and MSSPs can use Ameen as a recurring compliance readiness service.

Best use case:
- Monthly compliance readiness dashboard.
- Evidence follow-up.
- Framework gap monitoring.
- Executive reporting.

### Priority 4: Technology Partners

Technology partners can strengthen deployment and trust.

Examples:
- Saudi cloud providers.
- Private cloud providers.
- System integrators.
- Data center operators.
- Secure hosting partners.

---

## 3. Channels to Avoid Initially

Ameen should avoid:
- Open marketplace selling before legal and security maturity.
- Low-touch public signup for regulated customers.
- Broad resellers with no compliance expertise.
- Partners that want to rebrand the product too early.
- Partners that push Ameen to claim certification or regulator approval.

---

## 4. Channel Model

### Direct Sales Model

Ameen sells, onboards, and supports the customer directly.

Best for:
- First pilots.
- Sensitive government accounts.
- Strategic enterprise accounts.
- Accounts requiring customer-controlled deployment.

### Co-Sell Model

Ameen and partner sell together.

Best for:
- Consultants with existing client relationships.
- System integrators bidding for compliance projects.
- Customers needing advisory plus platform.

### Partner-Led Delivery Model

Partner delivers advisory work using Ameen.

Ameen provides:
- Product license.
- Partner enablement.
- Technical support.
- Approved messaging.
- Delivery guardrails.

### Managed Readiness Model

Partner uses Ameen as a monthly service.

Customer buys a recurring readiness service rather than only software.

---

## 5. Partner Qualification Rules

A partner must have:
- Clear Saudi compliance/GRC capability.
- Named delivery owner.
- No history of exaggerated compliance claims.
- Ability to follow Ameen legal and trust language.
- Willingness to use approved sales material.
- Ability to protect customer data.

A partner must not:
- Promise certification.
- Claim NCA approval unless formally obtained.
- Upload customer data into external AI without customer approval.
- Present AI outputs as audit conclusions.
- Modify Ameen reports without disclosure.

---

## 6. Commercial Models

### Referral

Partner introduces the customer. Ameen sells directly.

Best for early phase.

### Resale

Partner sells Ameen as part of a broader proposal.

Use only after pricing, contract, and support model are mature.

### Services Bundle

Partner sells consulting services, and Ameen is included as the assessment platform.

Best for readiness projects.

### Managed Service

Partner sells Ameen-powered continuous readiness monitoring.

Best for recurring revenue.

---

## 7. Channel Success Metrics

Track:
- Number of qualified partner leads.
- Number of active co-sell opportunities.
- Pilot conversion rate by channel.
- Average sales cycle by channel.
- Partner-influenced revenue.
- Customer satisfaction by partner.
- Number of partner delivery issues.
- Number of trust or compliance messaging violations.

---

## 8. First 90-Day Channel Plan

### Days 1-30

- Finalize direct pilot offer.
- Prepare partner one-pager.
- Identify 10 high-quality Saudi consulting/MSP partners.
- Start conversations with 3 trusted partners only.

### Days 31-60

- Run 1 co-sell pilot with a trusted consultant.
- Create partner demo environment.
- Create partner enablement pack.
- Document delivery guardrails.

### Days 61-90

- Formalize first partner agreement.
- Publish partner onboarding checklist.
- Build joint proposal template.
- Decide whether to expand to more partners.

---

## 9. Strategic Rule

Ameen must never give partners control over the product truth.

Partners may sell and deliver services around Ameen, but the following remain controlled by Ameen:
- Framework status.
- AI trust language.
- Readiness methodology.
- Product roadmap.
- Legal disclaimers.
- Report language.
- Data handling standards.

---

## 10. Final Position

The best near-term channel is:

> Direct pilots first, then trusted consulting co-sell, then MSP managed readiness.

Ameen should build channel revenue slowly and carefully, because one poor partner can damage trust faster than ten good features can build it.
