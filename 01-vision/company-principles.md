# Ameen Company Principles

These principles define how Ameen thinks, builds, sells, and supports customers.

They are not slogans. They are decision rules.

## 1. Trust Before Features

A compliance product without trust has no value.

Ameen must never optimize for feature count at the expense of accuracy, traceability, security, or customer confidence.

If a feature makes the product look impressive but weakens trust, we do not build it.

## 2. Official Sources First

Official Saudi regulatory publications are the source of truth for regulatory requirements.

Ameen must never invent, paraphrase, translate, or reinterpret official requirements as if they were regulation.

Ameen may provide guidance, explanation, examples, and evidence recommendations, but these must be clearly separated from official regulatory content.

## 3. AI Assists, Humans Decide

AI may analyze documents, suggest mappings, identify gaps, recommend evidence, and draft improvements.

AI must not approve compliance, verify evidence, certify readiness, or claim regulatory acceptance.

Readiness must require human review where human judgment is necessary.

## 4. Explain Every Result

Ameen must avoid black-box compliance scores.

Every important result should be traceable to:

- The official control.
- The customer document.
- The matched passage or evidence.
- The AI rationale.
- The human reviewer decision.
- The audit trail.

A score without explanation is not enough.

## 5. Simple Outside, Powerful Inside

The customer journey must remain simple.

Customers should not need to understand framework packaging, extraction pipelines, evidence models, AI routing, or scoring algorithms.

The product should feel like:

> Upload documents. Review findings. Improve readiness.

Complexity belongs inside the engine, not in the customer experience.

## 6. Saudi First

Ameen's first market is Saudi Arabia.

The product must deeply understand Saudi compliance requirements, Arabic language needs, and local customer expectations before expanding broadly.

Saudi depth is more important than global breadth at this stage.

## 7. Be Honest About Limits

Ameen must never overstate readiness, legal standing, AI accuracy, framework coverage, or audit acceptance.

If something is unverified, say it is unverified.

If something requires legal review, say it requires legal review.

If something requires human judgment, say it requires human judgment.

## 8. Measure Before Claiming

Every major product claim should be backed by evidence.

Examples:

- Mapping accuracy should be measured.
- Arabic parity should be measured.
- Framework completeness should be verified.
- Security claims should be tested.
- Performance claims should be benchmarked.

Claims without measurement are marketing, not trust.

## 9. Do Not Build What We Should Integrate

Ameen should not become a SIEM, EDR, scanner, ticketing system, CMDB, or identity platform.

Ameen should integrate with those systems and use them as evidence sources.

The product should focus on compliance intelligence, evidence reasoning, readiness, and governance.

## 10. Product Before Project

Ameen is not a one-off implementation.

Every decision should strengthen the reusable platform, the Saudi compliance knowledge base, the customer journey, and the company's long-term defensibility.

## 11. Design for Pilots, Then Enterprise

The first commercial motion is paid pilots with design partners.

Enterprise-grade deployment, procurement, legal, support, and security requirements must be built deliberately, but pilot learning should start before the product becomes perfect.

## 12. Protect the Customer

Compliance data is sensitive.

Customer documents, evidence, gaps, and readiness results must be treated as confidential business-critical information.

Security, privacy, tenant isolation, AI-provider transparency, and deployment control are core product requirements, not optional enhancements.
