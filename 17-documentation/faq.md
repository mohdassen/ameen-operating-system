# Ameen Customer FAQ

Status: Draft v1.0  
Owner: Product / Sales / Customer Success  
Audience: Prospects, customers, partners

---

## What is Ameen?

Ameen is a Saudi AI Compliance Readiness Platform. It helps organizations analyze existing documents, map them to selected compliance controls, identify gaps, organize evidence, and generate readiness dashboards and reports.

---

## Is Ameen a GRC platform?

Ameen can support GRC teams, but its first position is more specific: document-driven compliance readiness.

Traditional GRC platforms often focus on registers, workflows, risk libraries, and manual control tracking. Ameen starts from the customer's existing documents and evidence, then helps interpret readiness using AI and explainable mapping.

---

## Does Ameen certify compliance?

No.

Ameen does not certify compliance, issue regulatory approvals, or replace an auditor. It helps the organization prepare, understand gaps, organize evidence, and improve readiness.

---

## Does Ameen replace consultants?

Not completely.

Ameen can reduce manual effort and improve consistency, but consultants may still be needed for interpretation, remediation, operating model design, and formal advisory work.

Ameen can also be used by consultants to deliver faster and more traceable assessments.

---

## Which frameworks does Ameen support?

Ameen's first commercial focus is Saudi cybersecurity and governance readiness, starting with NCA-related frameworks and expanding carefully.

The product must always distinguish between frameworks that are:

- Available for customer use.
- Imported but not yet published.
- Planned on the roadmap.

Ameen must not present a planned framework as active.

---

## How does the AI work?

Ameen analyzes uploaded documents and suggests possible control mappings. It can explain why a document appears relevant to a control.

The AI does not approve compliance. Human review remains required.

---

## Can Ameen work without sending documents to an external AI provider?

The recommended sensitive-customer pilot model is customer-controlled deployment with local analysis by default.

External AI provider usage must be clearly disclosed and should require customer approval.

---

## Is customer data used to train public AI models?

Ameen's customer-facing position should be that customer data handling depends on the agreed deployment and AI-provider model.

For sensitive pilots, the safest model is local analysis or customer-approved provider configuration. Any claim about model training must be verified against the actual provider terms and deployment architecture before being used in a contract or website.

---

## What documents should customers upload?

Customers should upload approved documents related to the assessment scope, such as:

- Security policies.
- Procedures.
- Standards.
- Risk registers.
- Incident response plans.
- Business continuity documents.
- Vendor security documents.
- Evidence artifacts.

Customers should avoid uploading unnecessary sensitive data outside the agreed scope.

---

## Can Ameen analyze Arabic documents?

Ameen is positioned as Arabic-first, but every release must be measured honestly. Arabic analysis quality should be tested and reported rather than assumed.

If Arabic quality has known limitations in a release, those limitations should be disclosed internally and fixed before broad commercial claims.

---

## What is a readiness score?

A readiness score summarizes how much of the selected framework appears documented, implemented, evidenced, and verified.

The score is a readiness indicator, not a legal certification.

---

## Why does Ameen separate documented, implemented, evidenced, and verified?

Because having a policy is not the same as operating a control.

This separation gives a more honest view of readiness and prevents false confidence.

---

## Who should use Ameen?

Primary users include:

- CISOs.
- Compliance managers.
- GRC teams.
- Risk managers.
- Internal audit teams.
- Control owners.
- Consultants.
- Managed service providers.

---

## What is the best first use case?

The best first use case is a controlled readiness pilot for one organization, one framework scope, and a limited document set.

This allows Ameen to prove value without overpromising.

---

## What should customers expect from a pilot?

A pilot should show whether Ameen can:

- Analyze real documents.
- Suggest useful mappings.
- Identify meaningful gaps.
- Improve evidence collection.
- Produce understandable dashboards.
- Support compliance discussions.

---

## Can Ameen be deployed for government or banks?

Government and banking customers require a conservative deployment model, strict data controls, security review, and legal/commercial approval.

Ameen should not be sold into sensitive production environments until the required security, legal, and operational readiness gates are satisfied.

---

## FAQ Principle

Every answer must protect trust.

Never claim certification, regulatory endorsement, legal assurance, or production readiness unless those claims are formally proven and approved.
