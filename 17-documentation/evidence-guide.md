# Ameen Evidence Guide

Status: Draft v1.0  
Owner: Product / Customer Success  
Audience: Compliance leads, control owners, evidence reviewers, consultants

---

## Purpose

This guide defines how evidence should be collected, reviewed, and interpreted inside Ameen.

Ameen's value depends on evidence quality. Weak evidence creates false confidence. Strong evidence creates trust.

---

## What Evidence Means

Evidence is any approved artifact that helps prove a control is documented, implemented, operating, or reviewed.

Evidence may include documents, screenshots, logs, reports, system exports, meeting minutes, approvals, tickets, configuration snapshots, or third-party attestations.

---

## Evidence vs Policy

A policy explains what the organization intends to do.

Evidence shows what the organization actually did or currently does.

Ameen must keep this distinction clear.

Example:

- Password Policy = documented.
- System password configuration export = implemented/evidenced.
- Access review sign-off = verified.

---

## Evidence Quality Criteria

Good evidence should be:

### Relevant

It directly supports the control being assessed.

### Current

It reflects the correct assessment period.

### Specific

It clearly shows the system, process, team, owner, or control being evaluated.

### Traceable

It can be linked back to a document, system, ticket, owner, date, or approval.

### Complete

It covers the requirement sufficiently, not just a small unrelated part.

### Approved

It is permitted for use in the assessment and follows the customer's data-handling rules.

---

## Evidence Strength Levels

Ameen should guide customers using practical strength levels.

### Strong Evidence

Strong evidence clearly proves the control exists and is operating.

Examples:

- Approved policy plus implementation record.
- System configuration export.
- Access review report with approval.
- Incident exercise report with lessons learned.
- Backup test result with date and owner.
- Risk register with treatment decisions.

### Medium Evidence

Medium evidence supports the control but may require additional proof.

Examples:

- Procedure document without recent execution record.
- Screenshot without date or owner.
- Meeting minutes mentioning the activity.
- Vendor confirmation without internal review.

### Weak Evidence

Weak evidence is not enough to rely on alone.

Examples:

- Generic policy text.
- Old screenshot.
- Email discussion without approval.
- Document with no owner or date.
- Evidence that only contains a keyword.

---

## Evidence Review Questions

Reviewers should ask:

- Does this evidence really support the selected control?
- Does it show design only, or actual operation?
- Is it from the correct time period?
- Does it belong to the correct organization or system?
- Is there a named owner?
- Is there approval or verification?
- Would this stand up in a serious readiness review?

---

## Evidence Lifecycle

Evidence should follow a controlled lifecycle:

1. Collected.
2. Uploaded.
3. Mapped to one or more controls.
4. Reviewed.
5. Accepted or rejected.
6. Included in readiness calculation.
7. Refreshed when expired or outdated.

---

## Evidence Expiry

Some evidence becomes outdated quickly.

Examples:

- Access reviews.
- Vulnerability reports.
- Backup test results.
- Incident response exercises.
- Risk registers.
- User lists.
- Configuration exports.

Ameen should support the concept that evidence may need refresh, even if it was once valid.

---

## Evidence Mapping Rules

A single evidence item may support multiple controls, but Ameen should avoid overclaiming.

Evidence should not be mapped to a control simply because it contains a related word.

Mapping should be based on meaning, context, and reviewer approval.

---

## Human Reviewer Role

The human reviewer is the final gate.

AI may recommend evidence, but AI should not verify it independently for compliance assurance.

Ameen's trusted position depends on this principle.

---

## Evidence Principle

The goal is not to increase the score.

The goal is to create a readiness view the organization can defend.
