# Ameen Support Model

## Document Status

Owner: Customer Success / Operations  
Applies to: Pilot and early enterprise customers  
Version: 1.0  
Status: Draft operating standard

---

## Purpose

This document defines how Ameen should support customers during pilots and early commercial deployments.

The support model must reflect Ameen's current market stage: trust-first, controlled, and honest about what is assured and what is not yet assured.

---

## Support Philosophy

Ameen support is not only technical troubleshooting.

Ameen support must help customers answer three questions:

1. What does this readiness result mean?
2. Why did Ameen reach this conclusion?
3. What should we do next?

The support team must combine product understanding, compliance understanding, and customer empathy.

---

## Support Principles

### 1. Trust Before Speed

A fast wrong answer damages Ameen more than a slower verified answer.

When support is unsure, the correct response is:

> We will verify this and come back with a confirmed answer.

### 2. Never Overstate Compliance

Support must never tell a customer they are compliant, certified, audit-ready, or approved by a regulator unless a formal authorized human assessment supports that statement.

Preferred language:

> Ameen indicates that this control appears supported by the uploaded document and evidence, subject to customer and auditor review.

### 3. Explain the Evidence

Every support response about readiness must point back to evidence, not opinion.

### 4. Protect Customer Data

Support must not request sensitive files through informal channels. Customer data handling must follow the approved pilot agreement and data handling statement.

### 5. Close the Loop

Every support issue must end with one of the following outcomes:

- Resolved.
- Workaround accepted.
- Product defect logged.
- Customer decision required.
- Out of scope.

## Support Tiers

### Tier 1: Product Usage Support

Typical issues:

- Login issues.
- Navigation questions.
- Upload guidance.
- How to read dashboard results.
- How to review AI mappings.
- How to upload evidence.

Owner:

- Customer Success.

Expected response:

- Same business day during pilot support hours.

---

### Tier 2: Compliance Interpretation Support

Typical issues:

- Why a document mapped to a control.
- Why evidence is considered weak.
- What type of evidence is expected.
- How to interpret readiness dimensions.
- Difference between documented, implemented, evidenced, and verified.

Owner:

- Customer Success with Compliance/Product review.

Expected response:

- Within agreed pilot SLA.

Important rule:

Ameen may explain product reasoning. It must not issue legal or regulatory certification opinions.

---

### Tier 3: Technical Support

Typical issues:

- Deployment issues.
- Data import/export problems.
- Analyzer behavior defects.
- Performance issues.
- Access control problems.
- Security concerns.

Owner:

- Engineering / Technical Operations.

Expected response:

- Based on severity.

---

### Tier 4: Security or Data Incident

Typical issues:

- Suspected unauthorized access.
- Data exposure concern.
- Cross-tenant behavior.
- Evidence access concern.
- Credential/key leakage.

Owner:

- Security owner, Engineering owner, Executive owner.

Expected response:

- Immediate internal escalation.

Rules:

- Do not speculate.
- Preserve logs.
- Contain first.
- Confirm impact before communicating final conclusions.
- Communicate transparently once verified.

---

## Severity Levels

### Severity 1: Critical

Definition:

A security, data exposure, or complete service outage affecting an active customer environment.

Response target:

- Immediate acknowledgement.
- Executive escalation.

Examples:

- Suspected unauthorized data access.
- Customer cannot access the system during agreed critical review window.
- Evidence or documents visible to wrong organization.

---

### Severity 2: High

Definition:

A major function is not working and blocks pilot progress.

Examples:

- Uploads failing.
- AI mapping unavailable.
- Dashboard not generating.
- Customer cannot review mappings.

---

### Severity 3: Medium

Definition:

A product issue affects usability but does not block the pilot.

Examples:

- UI confusion.
- Unexpected wording.
- Weak explanation quality.
- Export formatting issue.

---

### Severity 4: Low

Definition:

Question, enhancement, or minor issue.

Examples:

- Feature request.
- Terminology suggestion.
- Additional report format request.

---

## Pilot Support Hours

For early pilots, support should be positioned as guided professional support, not 24x7 managed service.

Recommended pilot language:

> Support is provided during agreed business hours for onboarding, product usage, readiness interpretation, and pilot execution. Critical security or data concerns may be escalated immediately through the agreed escalation channel.

---

## Support Channels

Recommended approved channels:

- Named email support channel.
- Shared pilot tracker.
- Scheduled weekly review call.
- Emergency escalation contact for security/data concerns.

Avoid:

- WhatsApp as official evidence exchange.
- Personal email for customer documents.
- Untracked verbal support commitments.

---

## Weekly Pilot Support Rhythm

During pilots, Ameen should hold a weekly support and progress meeting.

Agenda:

1. Current pilot status.
2. Documents uploaded.
3. Controls mapped.
4. Evidence added.
5. Open support issues.
6. Product defects.
7. Customer decisions needed.
8. Next week actions.

---

## Support Response Template

Every customer support response should include:

- Summary of issue.
- Current status.
- What we checked.
- Explanation in plain language.
- Customer action required, if any.
- Ameen action required, if any.
- Next update time.

---

## Escalation Rules

Escalate immediately if:

- Data access issue is suspected.
- Customer disputes readiness logic in an executive setting.
- The issue may affect trust in official framework content.
- AI produces misleading, unsafe, or unsupported output.
- Customer asks for certification or regulator-approved statement.
- Customer uploads highly sensitive information outside agreed pilot scope.

---

## What Support Must Not Do

Support must not:

- Certify compliance.
- Claim NCA endorsement.
- Give legal opinion.
- Modify assessment results manually to satisfy customer pressure.
- Accept uncontrolled sensitive data through informal channels.
- Promise roadmap dates without product approval.
- Blame AI without explaining what happened.

---

## Final Standard

Ameen support must make customers feel guided, protected, and respected.

The goal is not only to resolve tickets. The goal is to build trust in every assessment.
