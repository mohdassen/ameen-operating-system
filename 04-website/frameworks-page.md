## Framework Support Model

Ameen should present framework maturity using four levels:

| Level | Meaning | Customer Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Published | Available for customer assessment | Available today |
| Verified Import | Imported and undergoing review | In validation |
| Acquired | Source collected but not ready | Planned |
| Roadmap | Target framework not yet implemented | Future roadmap |

Never show all frameworks as equally ready.

## Framework Page Sections

### 1. The Challenge

Saudi organizations often have policies, procedures, and technical documents, but they struggle to answer simple executive questions:

- Which controls are covered?
- Which controls are missing?
- What evidence is required?
- Who owns each gap?
- Are we ready for review?

Ameen turns this into a guided, explainable workflow.

---

### 2. How Ameen Handles Frameworks

Ameen does not treat frameworks as static checklists. It converts framework requirements into a practical readiness process:

1. Upload current documents.
2. AI maps documents to framework controls.
3. The customer reviews AI suggestions.
4. Ameen highlights missing controls.
5. The customer uploads evidence.
6. Reviewers validate evidence.
7. Ameen generates readiness dashboards and reports.

---

### 3. One Document, Multiple Frameworks

Future direction:

Ameen should allow one uploaded document to support multiple frameworks where appropriate.

Example:

An Access Control Policy may support requirements across ECC, CCC, and other frameworks.

Customer value:

- Reduce duplicate work.
- Reuse evidence.
- Maintain one compliance evidence library.
- Understand readiness across multiple lenses.

---

### 4. Human Review is Required

Ameen should always reinforce:

AI assists. Humans decide.

Ameen can suggest mappings, evidence, and gaps, but customer reviewers must approve final status.
