# Ameen Website Sitemap

## Website Objective

The website must make Ameen look like a serious Saudi enterprise SaaS company.

The website should not overwhelm visitors with technical details. It should quickly communicate:

- What Ameen does.
- Why Saudi organizations need it.
- Why the results can be trusted.
- How the product works.
- How to request a demo or join a pilot.

## Primary Website Goal

Convert qualified visitors into demo requests and pilot conversations.

1. Product

## Page Structure

### 1. Home

Purpose: Explain Ameen in less than 30 seconds.

Key sections:

- Hero: AI compliance for Saudi Arabia.
- Simple workflow: Upload documents → AI analysis → readiness dashboard.
- Trust pillars: official frameworks, explainable AI, human approval, traceability.
- Frameworks supported / planned.
- Who Ameen is for.
- Pilot program call to action.

Primary CTA: Request Demo.
Secondary CTA: Join Pilot Program.

### 2. Product

Purpose: Explain the product capabilities.

Sections:

- Document intelligence.
- Control mapping.
- Evidence management.
- Readiness dashboard.
- Human review workflow.
- Audit traceability.
- Knowledge layer.

CTA: See Ameen in action.

### 3. How It Works

Purpose: Explain the journey simply.

Sections:

1. Create organization.
2. Upload documents.
3. AI analyzes content.
4. AI maps controls.
5. Review and approve.
6. Upload evidence.
7. Track readiness.
8. Improve continuously.

CTA: Request a guided demo.

### 4. Frameworks

Purpose: Show Saudi regulatory focus.

Sections:

- NCA ECC.
- NCA CCC.
- NCA TCC.
- NCA OSMACC.
- Future: DGA Qiyas, PDPL, SAMA, DCC, CSCC, OTCC.

Important: Clearly label availability status.

Avoid claiming support for frameworks that are not production-ready.

CTA: Discuss your framework requirements.

### 5. AI & Trust

Purpose: Explain why customers should trust AI outputs.

Sections:

- AI assists, humans decide.
- Official content is never generated by AI.
- Every result is explainable.
- Local analyzer option.
- Provider transparency.
- No black-box compliance claims.
- Human review before readiness changes.

CTA: Learn how Ameen protects trust.

### 6. Solutions

Parent page explaining target sectors.

Subpages:

- Government
- Higher Education
- Healthcare
- Financial Services
- Consulting Firms
- Managed Service Providers

Each page should explain the customer's specific pain, Ameen's value, and likely frameworks.

### 7. Pilot Program

Purpose: Recruit design partners.

Sections:

- What the pilot includes.
- Who should apply.
- Deployment model.
- What Ameen will and will not claim.
- Expected outcomes.
- Pilot timeline.
- Commercial benefits for founding design partners.

CTA: Apply for pilot.

### 8. Pricing

Purpose: Position pricing without locking early numbers publicly.

Sections:

- Professional.
- Enterprise.
- Government.
- Consultant / Partner.
- Paid Pilot.

Early stage recommendation: do not publish exact prices until after customer discovery.

CTA: Request pricing.

### 9. Resources

Purpose: Build credibility and organic discovery.

Content types:

- Saudi compliance guides.
- ECC readiness articles.
- AI compliance explainers.
- Evidence preparation guides.
- Regulatory updates.
- Product notes.

### 10. About

Purpose: Explain mission and philosophy.

Sections:

- Why Ameen exists.
- Saudi-first approach.
- Trust principles.
- What Ameen will never claim.

### 11. Contact / Request Demo

Purpose: Capture leads.

Form fields:

- Name.
- Organization.
- Role.
- Email.
- Phone.
- Sector.
- Framework interest.
- Preferred deployment model.
- Message.

## Website Voice

The website should sound:

- Clear.
- Confident.
- Trustworthy.
- Saudi-aware.
- Enterprise-ready.

It should not sound:

- Overhyped.
- Too technical.
- Like a chatbot.
- Like a generic cybersecurity company.

## Website Success Metrics

The website succeeds if visitors can answer these questions within two minutes:

1. What is Ameen?
2. Who is it for?
3. Which problem does it solve?
4. Why should I trust it?
5. What should I do next?
