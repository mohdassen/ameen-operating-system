# Ameen Pricing Strategy

**Document owner:** ChatGPT / Product & GTM  
**Status:** Draft v1.0  
**Purpose:** Define the first commercial pricing strategy for Ameen, focused on paid pilots and early Saudi enterprise adoption.

---

## Pricing Philosophy

Ameen should not compete as cheap software.

Ameen should be priced as:

> A trusted AI compliance readiness platform that reduces consulting effort, accelerates assessment, and improves executive visibility.

The pricing should reflect value, but the first commercial motion should reduce customer risk through paid pilots.

---

## What Customers Are Paying For

Customers are not paying only for access to software.

They are paying for:

- Faster readiness assessment
- Reduced manual document review
- Better control mapping
- Evidence guidance
- Executive reporting
- Saudi framework knowledge
- Traceability
- Confidence before audits
- Lower consulting dependency

---

## Do Not Price by User Initially

For early Saudi enterprise customers, user-based pricing can create friction.

Ameen should price primarily by:

- Organization size
- Framework scope
- Deployment model
- Number of assessed entities or business units
- Support level
- AI processing mode

Users should be generous inside each package so adoption is not blocked.

---

## Recommended Commercial Motion

### Stage 1 — Paid Design Partner Pilots

This is the correct first monetization step.

**Why:**

- Product is strong but still needs real customer validation.
- Paid pilots create commitment without requiring enterprise procurement confidence.
- Customer feedback improves the product.
- Case studies and references become possible.

### Stage 2 — Annual Subscriptions

After 3-5 successful pilots, convert to annual contracts.

### Stage 3 — Enterprise and Government Deployments

Dedicated deployments, support commitments, and stronger security terms.

---

## Pilot Pricing

### Founding Design Partner Pilot

**Duration:** 90 days  
**Target:** 3-5 Saudi organizations  
**Deployment:** customer-controlled infrastructure preferred  
**AI mode:** local analyzer by default unless customer approves external provider  
**Framework:** ECC first

### Suggested Price Range

**SAR 25,000 – 75,000 per pilot**

Use the lower end for:

- Small organizations
- Friendly design partners
- Customers providing valuable feedback and reference potential

Use the higher end for:

- Large organizations
- Complex scope
- Government or regulated customers
- Additional support requirements

## Pricing Rules

1. Do not race to the bottom.
2. Do not give unlimited enterprise access for pilot pricing.
3. Do not promise future frameworks for free unless contractually controlled.
4. Do not include external AI costs without a usage policy.
5. Keep pilot scope clear.
6. Offer discounts for references, case studies, and roadmap feedback.
7. Price based on value and deployment complexity.

---

## External AI Cost Policy

For commercial use, Ameen must avoid unlimited AI cost exposure.

Recommended options:

### Option A — Local Analyzer Default

Best for early pilots.

### Option B — Customer Provides AI Key

Best for enterprise customers that want control.

### Option C — Managed AI with Usage Limits

Best for smaller customers.

Contracts must define:

- Monthly AI usage allowance
- Overage handling
- Approved providers
- Data processing disclosure
- Customer responsibility for external AI approval

- Upload and analyze customer documents
- AI control mapping
- Gap analysis
- Evidence readiness review
- Executive report
- Final pilot findings session

- Gap report
- Evidence recommendations
- Pilot conclusion report

---

## Success Metric for Pricing

A pilot is successful if the customer says:

> This saved us weeks of manual review and gave us a clearer readiness picture than our current process.

Only after that should enterprise pricing be pushed aggressively.

---

## Current Recommendation

Start with **paid pilots**, not full enterprise subscriptions.

Ameen is strong enough to charge for serious evaluation, but the first commercial goal should be proof, references, and customer trust — not maximum revenue.
