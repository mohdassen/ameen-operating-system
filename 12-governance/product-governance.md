# Ameen Product Governance

**Document owner:** Product Leadership  
**Status:** Working Draft  
**Audience:** Founder, Engineering, Design, Sales, Customer Success

---

## Purpose

Product governance defines how Ameen decides what to build, what to delay, and what to reject.

Ameen must avoid becoming a scattered product with many screens and weak trust.

---

## Product Governance Principle

The customer journey is fixed:

> Create Organization → Upload Documents → AI Analyzes → AI Maps Controls → Customer Reviews → Upload Evidence → Readiness Dashboard

All product decisions must protect this journey.

---

## Decision Authority

### Founder / CEO

Owns:

- Company direction
- Commercial priorities
- Strategic customer decisions
- Pricing approval
- Partnership decisions
- Risk acceptance

### Product Leadership

Owns:

- Product strategy
- Customer journey
- Roadmap prioritization
- Feature definitions
- Product messaging
- Release readiness criteria

### Engineering Leadership

Owns:

- Architecture
- Security implementation
- QA execution
- Technical feasibility
- Code quality
- Production readiness evidence

### Compliance Reviewers

Own:

- Regulatory content review
- Arabic clause review
- Evidence expectation review
- Control interpretation review
- Advisory guidance quality

### Customer Success

Owns:

- Onboarding experience
- Support workflow
- Training material
- Customer feedback capture
- Pilot success measurement

---

## Product Decision Types

### Type 1: Strategic Decisions

High-impact decisions that affect company direction.

Examples:

- Target market change
- New regulatory category
- New deployment model
- Major pricing change
- Public launch approval

These require founder approval and a written decision record.

### Type 2: Product Experience Decisions

Decisions that affect customer journey, UX, or feature behavior.

Examples:

- Changing assessment flow
- Adding a new review step
- Changing dashboard scoring
- Modifying report language

These require product approval and customer impact review.

### Type 3: Engineering Decisions

Decisions that affect architecture, security, QA, or technical implementation.

Examples:

- Encryption design
- AI provider isolation
- Database migration
- Performance limits
- Test strategy

These require engineering approval and evidence.

### Type 4: Content Decisions

Decisions that affect regulatory clauses, guidance, examples, or evidence expectations.

Examples:

- Publishing a new framework
- Changing control interpretation
- Adding advisory guidance
- Translating regulatory content

These require compliance review and source traceability.

---

## Product Acceptance Criteria

A feature is not done until it satisfies:

1. Customer value is clear.
2. Customer journey remains simple.
3. Security impact is reviewed.
4. QA is executed.
5. Regression risk is checked.
6. Commercial claim is safe.
7. Documentation is updated.
8. Support impact is understood.

---

## Forbidden Product Behavior

Ameen must not:

- Claim certification.
- Claim regulator endorsement without proof.
- Automatically approve compliance.
- Hide AI uncertainty.
- Mix official regulation with advisory guidance without labels.
- Add heavy workflow that makes the product feel like a generic GRC tool.
- Add frameworks faster than they can be verified.

---

## Product Review Questions

Before any major product change, answer:

1. What customer problem does this solve?
2. Does it improve trust or simplicity?
3. What risk does it introduce?
4. What must be tested?
5. What must be documented?
6. What must sales not overclaim?
7. What must support be ready to answer?

---

## Governance Rule

Ameen should move fast, but not carelessly.

In compliance software, trust lost is harder to recover than speed lost.
